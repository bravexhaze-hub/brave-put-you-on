So how the hell is this legal?

In 2018, Congress passed the Farm Bill and created a federal legal category called hemp.

Instead of separating “hemp” and “marijuana” based simply on whether the plant could get you high, the federal definition focused on one particular measurement: delta-9 THC concentration.

Under the federal definition currently in effect, cannabis and cannabis derivatives can qualify as hemp if they contain no more than 0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight.

What is THCA?

Cannabis flower can contain a lot of THCA while containing relatively little delta-9 THC.

THCA itself is not the same molecule as delta-9 THC. But when you apply heat:

THCA → HEAT → Δ9 THC

That process is called decarboxylation.

One last thing

Congress has already changed the federal definition of hemp again.

Congress originally scheduled the new federal hemp restrictions to take effect on November 12, 2026, but that deadline has since been delayed to December 11, 2026. The changes are designed to account for total THC, including THCA, along with additional restrictions on certain hemp-derived cannabinoid products.
